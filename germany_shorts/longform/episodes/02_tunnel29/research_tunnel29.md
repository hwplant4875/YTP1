## A2. Why they dug

- **Spina and Sesta**: in February 1962 their East Berlin friend Peter Schmidt asked for help. He faced conscription and had a wife and a baby a few weeks old (S8). S3 says "to help a fellow student and his family escape". S2 says the Italians "promised an East Berlin family a way out". [VERIFIED in outline]
- **Herschel**: his sister Anita Moeller, her husband Hannes and daughter Astrid (S15, S16, S14, S13). [VERIFIED] Astrid's age: "one year old" (S15). A visitberlin page seen only in search results said 18 months and Anita 22 [UNVERIFIED]. Astrid Nora Moeller later made the documentary *Der Fluchthelfer – Wege in die Freiheit* (2011) (S2, S16).
- **Herschel's backstory**: took part in the 17 June 1953 uprising, then was arrested in 1955 for "economic crimes" (S13, S16). Sentence: 5 years (S13, S16), "six years" (S14), "4½-year stint in a labor camp" (S15). Part of it was spent building the Schwarze Pumpe plant (S16). [DISPUTED length]
- **Rudolph**: S9 quotes him saying the border was "ein rotes Tuch für jeden" ("a red rag to everyone"). The 2009 plaque says the tunnel was dug "by brave men, so that they could take their wives, children, relatives and friends in their arms again" (S11).
- **Peter Fechter**: the 18-year-old shot and left to bleed to death at the Wall on 17 Aug 1962, during the dig. The diggers pinned his photo at the tunnel entrance (S5). S8 says Rudolph hung it. [VERIFIED]

## A3. The route: factory to cellar

- **Entrance (West, Wedding, French sector)**: cellar of a small, war-damaged factory at **Bernauer Straße 78** (S2, S8, S10, S11). S1 says "Bernauer Straße 78/79". S8 says it was near the corner of Wolgaster Straße, about 30 m from the sector border, and that the owner (Müller) lent the cellars rent-free with electricity. The factory is described as a "swizzle-stick factory" (S3, S6) or "cocktail-straw factory" (S12). Merriman lists the location as "Wolgaster Strasse" and says "the building no longer exists" (S12). [VERIFIED as Bernauer Str. 78, on the corner with Wolgaster Str.]
- **Original target**: a cellar on **Rheinsberger Straße** (key copied from a Bulgarian engineer's flat during a birthday party, S8). It was abandoned because of water (S8, S9). [VERIFIED that Rheinsberger Str. was the original target, S8 + S9]
- **Actual exit (East, Mitte)**: cellar of **Schönholzer Straße 7** (S1, S2, S10, S11, S12). S3 calls it "a deserted apartment building… ironically, vacated by the East German police because it was too close to the Wall". [SINGLE on the "vacated by police" detail] Escapees climbed up "through a broken cellar ceiling" (S10).
- **Plaque**: at Schönholzer Straße 7 since 14 Sep 2009 (Berliner Unterwelten e.V.). It says 135 m and 29 people on 14 and 15 Sep 1962. It adds that at least twelve tunnels were begun around Bernauer Straße and only three succeeded, the others "mostly through betrayal" (S11).
- **Dimensions**: about 80 cm wide and about 120 cm high, with rails and lamps (S10). S3 says "only three feet by three feet". S8 gives a 90 cm wide rectangular frame. S9 says less than one metre high.
- **Engineering**: about 20 tonnes of timber (S3; S9 citing *Der Spiegel* 1962), and about 250 tonnes of earth removed (S9). There was an electric cart on rails, electric lighting, stovepipe ventilation and pumps (S3, S8, S10). Seven cellar rooms were filled with spoil (S10). The timber came from West Germany (S10). S8 says it was a 10,000 DM donation via a sawmill. Telephone: a Wehrmacht field telephone (S8). Diggers lay on their backs (S8), and NBC filmed "mostly lying on their backs" (S5).
- **Length**: see Disputed. 135 m is the plaque figure and the most-cited.
- **Depth**: see Disputed (about 4–6 m in most German sources, about 12 m in S13).

## A4. Timeline of the dig

- **Feb 1962**: Peter Schmidt asks the Italians for help. They consider a helicopter, then choose a tunnel (S8).
- **Late March 1962**: preparations complete and the core team assembled (Sesta, Spina, Schrödter, Rudolph, Herschel, Pfeifer, "der Lange") (S8). S3 says "work… began in March 1962". S5 and S15 say May. S2 and S10 say "early summer" [DISPUTED].
- **Funds**: they started with only 1,500 DM (S8). Early donations came from political groups, anti-communist groups and newspapers, and ran out "before the tunnel was one-quarter done" (S3).
- **May 1962**: NBC deal struck (S4). S5 says June [DISPUTED].
- **Early June 1962**: they reach the demarcation line. As a joke or warning they hang a sign inside the tunnel reading "You are leaving the American sector" (S8, [SINGLE]).
- **June / early July 1962: burst water pipe**. S3 says work stopped in June "when a nearby water main broke", and again in July "when the tunnel was inundated by 40,000 gallons of water". S8 describes a 2 a.m. call in early July reporting 10 cm of water 15 m past the border line. They tried pumps, then talked the West Berlin (Wedding) building authority into asking the East to shut off the main, disguised as a routine repair. The tunnel took 2–3 weeks to dry, and the episode cost about four weeks (S8). Water was pumped constantly: "8,000 gallons a week" (S3). S10 says "30,000 litres by hand". [VERIFIED that flooding/pipe bursts happened (S2, S3, S8, S9); details SINGLE]
- **While flooded**: Herschel, Pfeifer and Rudolph went to dig on the Kiefholzstraße tunnel, which was betrayed (see A6) (S3, S8).
- **13 Aug 1962**: planned breakthrough on the Wall's first anniversary. Missed (S8).
- **17 Aug 1962**: Peter Fechter killed. His photo is pinned in the tunnel (S5, S8).
- **Aug–Sept 1962**: water again near Schönholzer Straße. Exit moved to Schönholzer Straße 7, and the date brought forward to 14 Sept (S8, S9). S5 says "the exit point was changed to throw off a suspected informant" [DISPUTED reason: water (S8, S9) vs informant (S5)]. NBC rented a top-floor room with a view of the Schönholzer Straße side (S8).
- **Duration**: "about four and a half months" (S10), "five months" (S13), "seven months" (S3, citing Herschel's "Seven Months to Dig a Tunnel" talk) [DISPUTED].

## A5. The NBC deal

- **How it started**: when money ran out in late May, Sesta and Spina answered an advertisement for a film about "Tunnel 28" and were passed via press contacts (Franz Baake, Fritjof Meyer) to NBC (S8, [SINGLE]). S3 says Anderton was known to be "looking for a tunnel" and Spina approached him. *Tunnel 28* was the German title of the Hollywood film *Escape from East Berlin* (dir. Robert Siodmak). It premiered in West Berlin on **22 Oct 1962**, based on a real January 1962 tunnel escape of 28 people (S23).
- **Money** [DISPUTED, see Section D]:
  - S3 (citing Wyden): Spina demanded **$50,000**, and Anderton bargained him down to **$12,000**.
  - S4: **$7,500** for exclusive filming access, plus **$5,000** if the escape succeeded (= $12,500 total, consistent with S3).
  - S5, S8, S10: **50,000 DM**. At the 1962 rate of about 4 DM per dollar (my arithmetic, not a source) that is about $12,500, again consistent. S10: about half went into construction and the rest was split among the three planners. S5 says about $20,000 of NBC money went into building and the rest was shared (internally inconsistent).
  - S2: Spina and Sesta each received 15,000 DM, plus TV rights for Germany and Italy. Herschel was included only after threatening to quit.
  - S8: Herschel was originally promised 2,000 DM. After the escape he learned NBC held German and Italian TV rights and worldwide stills rights (*Stern* bought the German photo rights). He pressured NBC and signed his own contract.
  - S7 gives "about $150,000 (2016 equivalent)". S5 gives "about $150,000 today" for 50,000 DM.
- **Backlash**: 17 former helpers publicly distanced themselves from Herschel, Sesta and Spina for making escape aid a business (S2, S8, S9, S10) [VERIFIED]. Most helpers did not know about the deals (S9).
- **NBC secrecy**: knowledge was "need to know". Frank kept Anderton and the cameramen away until near the escape. Frank "never saw the tunnel, and only drove by the swizzle-stick factory once, the night of the escape" (S3). S6 says he had the car drive past without slowing. NBC money was routed "outside the NBC channels" (S5). Coded messages went East over NBC's short-range radios (S5).
- **Footage**: about 12,000 feet of silent black-and-white film (S6). The film mixed real tunnel footage with later re-enactments by participants and had no interviews (S3). Diggers' faces were blurred (S8).

## A6. The rival CBS tunnel, the Stasi informer and the Kiefholzstraße trap

- A second 1962 tunnel at **Kiefholzstraße (Neukölln) to Puderstraße (Treptow)** was run by Fritz Wagner, Harry Seidel and the **Girrmann Group** ("Unternehmen Reisebüro") (S3). Its diggers offered **CBS** correspondent **Daniel Schorr** exclusive filming rights. CBS paid its organisers **$1,250** (S4).
- The US Mission in Berlin learned of it through James P. O'Donnell (NARA memo of 3 Aug 1962). It pressed O'Donnell, then Schorr, and Secretary of State **Dean Rusk** pressured the head of CBS News (S3, S4). CBS had planned to air on 13 Aug, the Wall's anniversary (S4).
- **7 Aug 1962**: as truckloads of escapees converged on the eastern exit, East German forces sprang a trap. Look-outs warned the diggers, including Herschel, Pfeifer and Rudolph, who fled back through the tunnel. **37** East Berliners waiting that night were arrested, and **52** more participants later (S3). S8 says "at least 102" were arrested and places it "mid-July" [DISPUTED date and count]. Merriman lists Puderstraße 7 (the house where the betrayed tunnel broke through) and the corner of Puderstraße and Herkomerstraße, where many were arrested (S12).
- **The informer**: S3 says a Stasi IM code-named **"Hardy"**, placed in the Girrmann Group at the end of March 1962, worked as a courier and "blew the tunnel". Kirkus (S18) says the Girrmann Group's East German messenger was the Stasi spy **Siegfried Uhse**. S26 (snippet) says the Kiefholzstraße tunnel was betrayed by Uhse. Merriman: Uhse met his handler at the "Orient" safe house (Torstraße 72) on the day he betrayed the operation. His flat was at Augsburger Str. 21, and he was originally caught smuggling cigarettes at Friedrichstraße station. His Stasi file includes his handwritten commitment letter (S12). Mitchell: Uhse's actions "led to the arrest of dozens and the shooting of one tunneler", and "there is no record that he ever expressed any regrets" (S19).
  - **Uncertain**: whether "Hardy" (S3) and Uhse are the same person is **not confirmed** by anything I read. Say "a Stasi informer" or "Siegfried Uhse, according to Mitchell and Merriman".
  - **Uncertain**: whether Uhse knew about Tunnel 29. A low-quality aggregator in search results claimed Uhse's knowledge led the Stasi to the plan but "on the night of the breakthrough the Stasi were not waiting". Merriman lists a "CIA house… where Mimmo told the Americans about the leak" (S12). S8 says a helper called **"Rolf"** inside the Tunnel 29 project was later revealed as a Stasi informant [SINGLE]. Do not state as fact that the Stasi knew of Tunnel 29 in advance.
  - S3 suggests that while the tunnel was flooded the Stasi "may have been convinced that it was doomed", and that the abandonment may have "saved the operation" (speculation in a NARA essay).
- **Stasi and the aftermath**: the Stasi learned of the escape from Western press reports. It found the tunnel **11 days later** after subsidence in a courtyard (S8 says Schönholzer Str. 17, possibly a typo for 7). S5 says it was "discovered when it collapsed 11 days after". [VERIFIED: 11 days, S5 + S8]
- No one was killed, injured or arrested in the Tunnel 29 operation (S2, S10). [VERIFIED]

## A7. US government pressure and the broadcast

- The State Department learned of NBC's film only in October 1962 (S3). *Time* revealed NBC's payment in early October and called it "chicanery" (S4).
- **11 Oct 1962**: NBC announced the broadcast for **31 Oct, 7:30–9 p.m.** EST (S7). On 22 Oct the *New York Times* critic Jack Gould called the payment "distasteful commercialism" (S4). That night Kennedy announced the Cuban missile crisis (S4).
- **23 Oct 1962**: NBC postponed indefinitely (S7). The NYT reported it on 24 Oct, "N.B.C. Postpones Tunnel Telecast" (S7). Reasons: State Department pressure (S3, S4, S5) and the West Berlin government's alarm (S3). Senator for the Interior Heinrich Albertz criticised the project (S8). Airmail says it was "under pressure from Bobby Kennedy" (S25). Robert F. Kennedy is reported to have said "That was a terrible thing you people did, buying that tunnel" (S7, flagged on Wikipedia as needing clarification). NBC president Robert Kintner sent a company attorney to West Berlin. Rusk said he still opposed it but the decision was NBC's (S4).
- **Aired Monday 10 Dec 1962**: *The Tunnel*, an NBC White Paper of 90 minutes, sponsored by Gulf Oil (S7). 13.5 million viewers (S4, [SINGLE]). The **USIA bought 100 copies** to show worldwide (S4, [SINGLE]). Note the irony: one arm of the US government tried to stop it, another distributed it.
- **Emmys (1963)**: three, including **Program of the Year**, the only documentary ever to win it. Reuven Frank won Outstanding Achievement in the Field of Documentary and Piers Anderton won Outstanding Achievement in International Reporting (S7). Three Emmys is also in S4, S5 and S19. [VERIFIED]

## A8. The escapees and afterwards

- **29 people** (all sources). S6 says "nearly thirty" and S5 "more than two dozen men, women and babies". S7 says "26 friends and family" [outlier]. A search snippet said "aged 8 to 80" [UNVERIFIED].
- Known escapees: Anita and Hannes Moeller with baby Astrid (S15, S16). Eveline (Evi) Schmidt, her baby, husband Peter and mother-in-law (S6, S8). Claus Störmer's wife and the son he had never met (S8). The last party: "three young women with four children" (S6).
- "One digger was reunited with his wife and newborn" (S5). The film's final scene shows "a tunneler holding his son for the first time" (S6). [VERIFIED as a fact. Identity Störmer per S8, SINGLE]
- One woman reportedly wore a **Dior dress** for her escape (S5, "reportedly"). Herschel's sister's dress was ruined (S8).
- Anderton planned to give his wife a child's doll, covered in dirt, that had been left in the tunnel (S6). Rudolph found shoes belonging to Evi's daughter in the tunnel (S12).
- **Love story**: Merriman says Evi (who escaped through the tunnel) later married Joachim Rudolph (S12). Merriman's site refers to "Evi" and "Evi's daughter". That she is the same person as Eveline Schmidt (Peter's wife in S6 and S8) is likely but **not explicitly confirmed** in what I read. Check before scripting.
- To protect the route, escapees were not taken to the Marienfelde refugee centre but housed privately (S8). Press reports of "59 refugees" were, per Arnold/Kellerhoff via S8, a cover story mixing in a Girrmann Group operation.
- **Later lives**: Herschel became a commercial escape helper (about 1,000 people by 1972, using cars with hidden compartments including a converted Cadillac, and passport swaps). He later ran restaurants and clubs, opened a club on 9 Nov 1989, and lived in the Uckermark from 1994 (S13, S15, S16). Rudolph and Herschel both received the Bundesverdienstkreuz in 2012 (S16, S17). Rudolph died on 28 Jan 2025 (S17). Herschel died on 21 May 2026 (S13, S14, S16). Spina and Sesta had died by about 2022 (S10).
- **The tunnel afterwards**: flooded and closed late on 15 Sept (S3, S9, S10). In September 2000 Spiegel TV had part of the intact tunnel uncovered, with participants helping to locate it (S8, S2 cites *Der Spiegel* 2000). A news report seen only as a search snippet dated a Spiegel TV find to December 2001 [DISPUTED year]. The Berlin Wall Memorial marks escape-tunnel lines in the ground along Bernauer Straße (photos in Part B). A 2022 housing project by WBM on Bernauer Str. 26/34 planned archaeological digs that might hit Tunnel 29 remains (S24). A replica with the original dimensions is at the Berliner Unterwelten museum, Brunnenstraße 105 (S12).
- **Screen versions**: *Der Tunnel* (Sat.1 TV film, 2001, Heino Ferch), loosely based on Herschel. Marcus Vetter's documentaries *Der Tunnel* (1999) and *Tunnel der Freiheit* (2020/21). BBC Radio 4 *Intrigue: Tunnel 29* (2019, 10 parts). Merriman's book (2021) (S2, S7, S10, S16, S21).

## B. Story hooks (surprising, cinematic, sourced)

1. **A TV network bought a hole under the Iron Curtain.** NBC paid the diggers (about $12,500 / 50,000 DM) for exclusive rights and filmed the escape from inside the tunnel. The producer never saw the tunnel and drove past the factory once, without slowing, on escape night (S3, S4, S6).
2. **Two networks, two tunnels, one betrayed.** CBS had its own tunnel at Kiefholzstraße. A Stasi informer gave it away, and on 7 Aug 1962 the trap closed. Three Tunnel 29 diggers (Herschel, Pfeifer, Rudolph) escaped only by crawling back underground (S3, S4, S18).
3. **Spycraft in a pub.** The courier, on her 22nd birthday, watched for a white sheet in a top-floor window (open) or a red one (danger). She signalled families by buying matches and carrying the evening *BZ*. The passwords were "Panzerkreuzer Potemkin" / "Eisenstein". For the last group the code was to order coffee in an East Berlin pub that had none, so she ordered cognac (S8, [SINGLE]; use "according to participants' accounts").
4. **Peter Fechter's photo on the tunnel wall.** While they dug, an 18-year-old bled to death at the Wall in full view of the West. The diggers pinned his picture at the entrance (S5, S8).
5. **The water kept coming.** A burst main forced a weeks-long halt. They talked the West Berlin building authority into getting the East to turn the water off under the cover of a repair. On the final night refugees crawled through rising water, "up to their faces" (S3, S8).
6. **"You are leaving the American sector."** The diggers hung the famous checkpoint sign inside the tunnel at the point where it crossed the border (S8, [SINGLE]).
7. **A father meets his son for the first time, on film.** The last scene NBC shot: a tunneller holding the baby he had never seen (S6; S5 "reunited with his wife and newborn").
8. **The film the White House tried to kill won Program of the Year.** It was postponed during the Cuban Missile Crisis and aired 10 Dec 1962 to 13.5 million viewers. It won three Emmys, still the only documentary to win Program of the Year, and the US government's own USIA then bought 100 prints (S4, S7).
Bonus: Hollywood's *Tunnel 28* premiered in West Berlin on 22 Oct 1962, the night of Kennedy's Cuba speech, while the real "Tunnel 29" film sat shelved (S4, S7, S23. The juxtaposition is mine but the dates are sourced). The Tunnel 29 team had first reached NBC by answering an ad for a "Tunnel 28" film (S8, [SINGLE]).

## D. Disputed / uncertain (summary)

1. **Length**: 120 m (S1, S16; Haack via S2), 126 m (*Der Spiegel* 2000 via S2), "more than 130 m" (S9), **135 m** (plaque S11, S10, S13, S12), 140 m (berlin.de via S2), "140 yards" ≈ 128 m (S3), "about 400 feet" ≈ 122 m (S6), "150 yards" (S15), "about 100 yards away" (S5). **Script: "about 135 metres" (plaque), or "well over 100 metres".**
2. **Depth**: "15 feet" ≈ 4.6 m (S3), 4–6 m planned / 4–5 m (S8), about 6 m (S10), about 12 m (S13). The 12 m figure looks like an outlier. **Script: "about five metres down".**
3. **Start date and duration**: March (S3), end of March (S8), May (S5, S15), "early summer" (S2, S10). 4.5 months (S10), 5 months (S13), 7 months (S3/Herschel).
4. **Money**: $12,000 (S3), $7,500 + $5,000 (S4), 50,000 DM (S5, S8, S10). Split: 15,000 DM each to Spina and Sesta (S2) vs half to construction and half to three planners (S10). These are broadly consistent at about 4 DM per $ (my arithmetic). **Script: "about $12,000, around 50,000 Deutschmarks".** Deal month: May (S4) vs June (S5).
5. **Who "led"**: German sources lead with Spina, Sesta and Herschel. English and BBC/Merriman lead with Joachim Rudolph. Both are true: the Italians initiated, and Rudolph was a key digger and engineer.
6. **Kiefholzstraße betrayal**: date 7 Aug 1962 (S3) vs "mid-July" (S8). Arrests 37 + 52 (S3) vs "at least 102" (S8). Informer "Hardy" (S3) vs Siegfried Uhse (S18, S26, Merriman). Identity link not confirmed.
7. **Did the Stasi know about Tunnel 29?** Unclear. Informer "Rolf" (S8). A "leak" reported to the Americans (S12). The exit change attributed to an informant (S5) vs water (S8, S9).
8. **Escapee count**: 29 (consensus), 26 (S7), press "59" (cover story per S8).
9. **Herschel's sentence**: 5 years (S13, S16), 6 years (S14), 4½ years served (S15), "four years" (S10).
10. **Spiegel TV rediscovery**: Sept 2000 (S8; S2 cites Spiegel 2000) vs Dec 2001 (unread news snippet).
11. **Who pressured NBC**: State Department / Rusk (S3, S4), Kennedy White House (S6, S19), "Bobby Kennedy" (S25). RFK quote flagged on Wikipedia (S7).
12. **Dehmel brothers as cameramen**: only S6 among what I read. Klaus Dehmel's role is described there in detail. I did not find a second independent source.
13. **Evi = Eveline Schmidt, and later Rudolph's wife**: Merriman (S12) says Evi married Joachim. Identity with Eveline Schmidt is not explicitly stated.
14. Escape-day clock times are almost entirely from S8 (single source).
