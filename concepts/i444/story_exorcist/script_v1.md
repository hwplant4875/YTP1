# The ENTIRE Story of The Exorcist (script v1, 2026-11-10)

New Horror Ranked series, benchmarked on Right Turn's "The ENTIRE Story of Jason Voorhees in 49 Minutes" (hJHNYzk1wW4).
Release order, one chapter per film. Present tense, fast and conversational, with small asides and trivia.
Every plot line was checked against the films' Wikipedia plot, production and reception sections on 11/10 (fact notes at the end).
Never show: the crucifix scene, any suicide act (Karras's fall, Bession, Dr. Temple, Granville, Sharon's fire), or sexualized content involving Regan, Angela or Katherine.

## Hook
How does one twelve-year-old girl with a Ouija board end up with a priest chasing a demon made of locusts across Africa, a serial killer hiding inside a dead priest's body, the same prequel being filmed twice by two different directors, and her own mother being blinded with a cross fifty years later? The Exorcist has six movies and a TV show, and half of them pretend the others never happened. So to understand how this story really goes, come along with us. It all starts in the desert of northern Iraq, with an old priest digging in the dirt.

## The Exorcist (1973)
Father Lankester Merrin is as much an archaeologist as he is a priest, and at a dig in the ancient ruins of Hatra, he pulls a small stone amulet out of the ground. It's the head of a winged creature, and the second he sees it, something in his face changes. Later he walks up to a giant statue of the same creature, and the two of them just stand there in the wind, staring at each other like two old enemies who know they'll meet again. The movie never says its name, but this is the demon Pazuzu, and it's about to find a much easier target.

Thousands of miles away, in Georgetown, Washington, D.C., actress Chris MacNeil is shooting a movie directed by her friend Burke Dennings. For the shoot she's renting a big house with her twelve-year-old daughter Regan and a small staff. Regan is sweet, funny and completely normal. Then she finds an old Ouija board in the house and starts playing with it by herself, and she tells her mom she's been talking to someone named Captain Howdy. Chris laughs it off, which, in a horror movie, is never a good sign.

Across town, Father Damien Karras is a Jesuit priest who also works as a psychiatrist, counseling the other priests at Georgetown University, and he's quietly losing his faith. His mother lives alone in New York, her health is failing, and soon she dies while he's away. Karras can't forgive himself for not being there.

Chris throws a party at the house. Late in the night, Regan comes downstairs in her nightgown, looks at one of the guests, an astronaut, and says, "You're gonna die up there." Then she wets herself in front of everyone. Chris puts her back to bed, and a few minutes later Regan's screams bring her running. The bed is shaking so violently that Chris has to throw herself on top of it.

What follows is one of the hardest parts of the movie to watch, and it has nothing to do with demons. The doctors put Regan through every test they have, needles, X-rays and a brain scan so realistic that, according to the director, it was later used in training films for radiologists. They find nothing. Meanwhile, Regan keeps getting worse. She's violent and foul-mouthed, she's far stronger than any twelve-year-old should be, and her voice isn't hers anymore. Out of options, one of the doctors brings up something strange, an exorcism. Not because he believes in demons, but because if Regan believes she's possessed, maybe believing she's been freed could work too.

One night, Chris comes home to a dark, empty house. Burke was supposed to be watching Regan, but she's alone, asleep upstairs. Burke is found dead at the bottom of a long, steep stone staircase right outside, the one that starts beneath Regan's window. Detective William Kinderman starts asking around, and he quietly tells Karras one detail he can't explain. When they found Burke, his head was turned all the way around. Then he tells Chris the only explanation that makes sense. Somebody pushed Burke out of Regan's window.

And the moment Kinderman leaves, Regan has her worst fit yet. She's scarred and bleeding, and in the middle of it her head turns all the way backwards and she speaks to her mother in Burke's voice. Chris doesn't need a doctor to tell her what that means. She goes looking for a priest, and she finds Karras.

Karras isn't convinced at first, because his job is to find the psychiatric explanation. Regan is now strapped to her bed, and the thing inside her claims to be the Devil himself. It vomits green bile straight into Karras's face, and it tells him it will stay inside Regan until she's dead. He tests it with a flask of holy water and it reacts violently, but later he admits to Chris that the flask was full of tap water, so maybe this is just a very sick child who knows what a possessed person is supposed to do. He records it speaking what sounds like gibberish, and a linguist realizes it's perfect English, spoken backwards. Then, one night, Chris's assistant Sharon calls him to the house and shows him Regan's stomach. Letters are rising up on her skin, from the inside, spelling two words. Help me.

That's enough for Karras. He asks his superiors for permission to perform an exorcism, and they say yes, on one condition. An experienced priest has to lead it. And the man they call is Father Merrin, who has done this before.

Merrin arrives at night in a taxi and steps out into the fog, a silhouette under a streetlight in front of the house, staring up at the light in Regan's window. That shot became the poster, and one of the most famous images in horror. The two priests start reading the Roman Ritual, and the demon throws everything it has at them. The room turns so cold they can see their breath, the bed shakes, the ceiling cracks, Regan rises into the air above the bed, and through all of it the two priests keep shouting the same line, over and over. The power of Christ compels you.

Merrin is old and has a bad heart, and during a break his hands are shaking as he takes his pills. Karras goes back into the room, and the demon takes the form of his dead mother. It's the one place where Karras is weakest, and the demon knows it. Merrin sends him out and goes on alone. Outside, Chris asks Karras if her daughter is going to die, and he promises her she won't. Then he walks back in and finds Merrin dead on the floor from a heart attack, with Regan watching him, laughing.

Karras loses it. He throws himself at the demon, fighting it with his bare hands and screaming at it to leave the girl and take him instead. The demon tears the Saint Joseph medal from his neck, and then it does exactly what he asked. Regan is free, and Karras feels the demon take hold of his own body. Before it can hurt her again, he makes his choice, and he goes through the window and down those same stone stairs. Father Dyer, his friend, reaches him at the bottom just in time to give him his last rites. He kept his promise.

Not long after, Chris and Regan leave Georgetown for good. Regan doesn't remember anything that happened. But when she sees Father Dyer's collar, she walks up to him and kisses him on the cheek, and before they drive away, Chris hands Dyer the medal she found in Regan's room.

The Exorcist became the first horror film ever nominated for Best Picture, with ten Oscar nominations and two wins, and it stayed the highest-grossing R-rated horror movie until It came out in 2017. The shoot ran twice as long as planned and cost almost three times its budget, with so many accidents and injuries that people still talk about it as a cursed production. And the demon's voice wasn't Linda Blair's at all. It belonged to an actress named Mercedes McCambridge. Two priests are dead, the girl is saved, and the story is over. At least, that's what everyone thought.

## Exorcist II: The Heretic (1977)
Exorcist II: The Heretic picks up four years later, and it's made by a completely different team, because the writer and the director of the original want nothing to do with it. They're too busy suing the studio and each other. You're about to see why that matters.

It opens with Father Philip Lamont, another priest with a shaky faith, trying to exorcise a possessed woman in Latin America who claims she can heal the sick. It goes horribly wrong. Lit candles get knocked over, her dress catches fire, and she dies. Then the Cardinal hands Lamont a new assignment. He has to investigate the death of Father Merrin, because the Church wants to put Merrin on trial for heresy over his writings, even though he's dead. The Church is trying to modernize, and it would rather not admit that Satan exists.

Regan is a teenager now, living in New York with Sharon. She seems normal and says she remembers nothing, but she's still being watched at a psychiatric institute, where Dr. Gene Tuskin is sure the memories are buried somewhere inside her. And the doctor has a machine to dig them out, a device called the synchronizer that links two people's brainwaves under hypnosis.

Tuskin won't let Lamont near Regan at first, but eventually the priest gets hooked up to the machine with her, and something pulls him into the past. Here the demon finally gets a name, Pazuzu, and it shows Lamont a younger Merrin in Africa, exorcising a little boy named Kokumo. The boy had developed special powers to fight Pazuzu, which shows up as a giant swarm of locusts.

So, against his superior's orders, Lamont flies to Africa to find Kokumo. The boy is a grown man now and, naturally, a scientist who studies how to stop locust swarms. Lamont learns that Pazuzu goes after people with psychic healing powers, and that Merrin believed people like Kokumo and Regan were the first of a new kind of human. Back in New York, Regan proves it. While she waits to see her doctor, she reaches into the mind of an autistic girl who can't speak, and the girl starts to talk.

In a vision, Merrin asks Lamont to watch over Regan, and the two of them go back to where it all started, the old house in Georgetown. On the way, Pazuzu offers Lamont unlimited power, speaking through an evil double of Regan. Tuskin and Sharon follow them in a taxi that crashes straight into the front of the house. The driver is killed, and Sharon doesn't make it through the night. Inside, a storm of locusts pours into the house as it starts to collapse around them. For a moment Lamont gives in, but the real Regan pulls him back, and he rips the heart out of her evil double with his bare hands. Then Regan finishes the job the way Kokumo tried to as a boy, with the same bullroarer ritual, until the locusts, and Pazuzu, are gone. Regan and Lamont walk away together.

The audience at the New York premiere laughed at it, and Blatty, the writer of the original, claimed he was the first person in his theater to start laughing. It's still regularly called one of the worst movies ever made, and William Friedkin, the director of the original, called it the worst piece of crap he had ever seen. So every movie that came after it did the only thing that made sense. They pretended it never happened.

## The Exorcist III (1990)
Thirteen years later, William Peter Blatty decides to make the real sequel himself. He adapts his own novel, Legion, directs it himself and simply skips Exorcist II. In The Exorcist III, it's been fifteen years since Regan's exorcism, and the hero is Lieutenant Kinderman, the detective from the first movie, now played by George C. Scott.

Kinderman and Father Dyer still get together to remember their friend Karras. Then strange things start happening in Georgetown. In a church, the eyes of the Christ figure on a crucifix suddenly open.

The next morning, Kinderman is called to a crime scene. Thomas Kintry has been murdered, and the killer took his head and put the head of a Jesus statue in its place. Then a priest, Father Kanavan, is killed inside his own confessional, beheaded the same way. Then Father Dyer falls ill, goes into the hospital and is murdered in his bed, with all of his blood drained into small bottles and lined up neatly. The fingerprints at the crime scenes don't match, as if a different killer did each one, but every detail matches the signature of one man, the Gemini Killer, a serial killer who was executed fifteen years ago. On the same night Karras died.

The trail leads Kinderman to the hospital's locked psychiatric ward. Dr. Temple, who runs it, tells him about a patient who was found wandering the streets fifteen years ago with no memory, and who stayed catatonic the whole time, until six weeks ago, when he suddenly woke up, violent, claiming to be the Gemini Killer. Kinderman walks into the cell and sees the face of his old friend. It's Damien Karras.

Kinderman goes to the priests for answers, and one of them spots the pattern. Every victim is tied to the Regan case. Kanavan was the priest who allowed the exorcism, Dyer was the family's friend, and Kintry's mother was the woman who decoded the backwards tape. The same priest tells him about a Father Morning, who once did an exorcism in the Philippines that supposedly turned his hair white overnight.

This movie also has one of the most famous scares in horror history, a long, static shot down a quiet hospital hallway, a nurse at her desk, nothing happening for what feels like forever, until it happens. That night the nurse is killed, and Dr. Temple takes his own life.

Back in the cell, the Gemini Killer explains everything himself, and he enjoys every second of it. When he was executed, a demon he calls the Master, almost certainly the same one Karras drove out of Regan, wanted revenge. So it took the killer's spirit and put it into Karras's body, with Karras still locked inside, forced to watch everything. And the Gemini doesn't even need to leave his cell. He takes over the elderly patients in the ward and uses their bodies to kill, and he forced Dr. Temple to help him.

Then one of those old patients leaves the hospital dressed as a nurse and goes to Kinderman's house to kill his family. The attack stops halfway, because back at the hospital, Father Morning has shown up on his own, sensing he's needed, to perform an exorcism on Karras. The Master takes over the body itself, and Morning is torn up badly. Kinderman runs into the cell with his gun, and the demon throws him around the room, but Morning, barely conscious, tells Karras to fight. For just one moment, the real Karras breaks through and begs his friend to shoot him. Kinderman does. Karras is finally free, and this time they bury him for real.

Here's the thing. Blatty's version had no exorcism at all. It ended with Kinderman simply shooting the patient. Then, months after filming wrapped, the studio told him to shoot a new ending, and according to Blatty, it was because the studio boss's secretary insisted there had to be an exorcism. Father Morning didn't even exist before that. He was invented just for the new climax. Blatty called it utterly unnecessary, but he still believed his movie was scarier than the original, and in 2016 a director's cut finally came out, rebuilt partly from old VHS tapes of the dailies, because the original footage was lost.

## Exorcist: The Beginning (2004)
Fourteen years later, the franchise goes back in time to answer one question. How did Merrin first meet this demon? Exorcist: The Beginning opens in the days of the Byzantine Empire, on a battlefield where a wounded priest walks through a desert covered with hundreds of soldiers crucified upside down.

Then we jump to Cairo in 1949. Merrin, now played by Stellan Skarsgård, isn't working as a priest anymore. Near the end of World War II, in a village in the occupied Netherlands, a sadistic Nazi officer named Kessel forced him to choose which villagers would be executed, or the whole village would die. Merrin chose, and his faith died with them.

An antiquities collector named Semelier offers him a job. In a remote valley called Derati, in British Kenya, a British team is digging up a Christian church from around the year 500, which shouldn't be possible, because Christianity hadn't reached that part of Africa yet. Semelier wants Merrin to find an ancient demon relic inside, before the British do.

The church is in perfect condition, as if someone buried it the moment it was finished. Inside, the statues of angels point their spears down at the floor instead of up to heaven, and someone has turned the cross upside down. Merrin and a young priest named Father Francis work out that the angels were carved holding something down. Something under the church.

The workers are quitting because the locals say the place is cursed. The lead archaeologist, Bession, went insane a few weeks earlier, and when Merrin visits him in a hospital in Nairobi, Bession has a swastika carved into his chest and speaks in the voice of Kessel, the Nazi who broke Merrin. Then, right in front of him, Bession ends his own life. Before Merrin leaves, a priest at the hospital hands him a book of exorcism rituals, and Merrin says he will never use it.

From there it gets worse, fast. A local boy is torn apart by hyenas that seem to stalk the dig, and his little brother Joseph goes silent and blank after seeing it. A baby is born dead, covered in maggots. Under the church, Merrin finds a tunnel to an ancient pagan temple with a statue of Pazuzu and signs of human sacrifice. And when he digs up a cemetery where a plague supposedly wiped out a village fifty years ago, the graves are empty.

Father Francis finally tells him the truth. Fifteen hundred years ago, an army led by two priests came to this valley looking for the source of evil, and evil got to them first. One priest killed the other. The Emperor ordered a church built over the spot and then buried, to seal in whatever was down there. In the 1890s, four priests came looking again and vanished along with the locals who helped them, so the Vatican invented the plague and the fake cemetery to keep people away. Some believe this valley is where Lucifer fell to Earth after the war in Heaven.

Later, Merrin and the British find the chief excavator, Jefferies, tied up inside the church, with crows picking at him. With Joseph silent and strange since his brother's death, you'd think he's the one the demon has taken. He isn't. It's Sarah, the doctor Merrin has been growing close to, and she turns out to be Bession's wife. She kills Father Francis, and in the tunnels under the church, Merrin finally opens the book he swore he'd never use and performs the exorcism. Sarah dies. When Merrin walks out with Joseph, the British soldiers and the local tribe have wiped each other out, and the church is buried in sand again, just like fifteen hundred years ago. Back in Rome, Merrin is a priest again, and when he tells Semelier he never found the relic, Semelier just answers, "But you found something, didn't you?"

And here's the crazy part. This was the second version of this movie. The studio already had an almost finished film by another director, Paul Schrader, but decided it wasn't scary enough. So it brought in Renny Harlin, rewrote the story, replaced almost the entire cast except Skarsgård, and shot it all again. Critics still hated it. It sits at eleven percent on Rotten Tomatoes, and Blatty called watching it his most humiliating professional experience.

## Dominion: Prequel to the Exorcist (2005)
And then, after The Beginning flopped, the studio did something almost no studio does. It released the version it had thrown away. Dominion: Prequel to the Exorcist came out in 2005, with the same Merrin and the same buried church in Kenya, but a different story.

This time it's 1947, Merrin is working on the dig as an archaeologist, and there's no Sarah. The doctor is Rachel Lesno, and the one who matters is Cheche, a disabled young outcast the locals stay away from. Merrin brings him to Rachel for treatment, and Cheche starts recovering much faster than he should. Too fast.

Inside the church they find the same kind of crypt, a demonic idol and the same signs of human sacrifice, and the local elders tell Merrin to stop digging. He doesn't. Two British soldiers sneak into the church to steal precious stones, and the next morning one is found without his head and the other crucified upside down on the altar. Major Granville, the British officer in charge, blames the locals and, in a fit of rage, shoots a young woman in cold blood. Violence spreads through the valley, and Granville, crushed by guilt, takes his own life.

Father Francis realizes Cheche's recovery isn't a miracle and wants to baptize him to save his soul. The boy agrees, but only if it happens inside the church. When they try, the demon inside Cheche attacks. Francis runs to get his book of rituals, but an earthquake seals the church shut. The next morning, Merrin finds Francis tied to a tree, shot full of arrows, and with his last breath Francis tells him that Cheche is possessed and begs him to do the exorcism.

So Merrin puts on Francis's robes and goes in. The demon offers him the one thing he wants most, a chance to go back to 1944 and undo what he did. In a vision, Merrin is back in that village, and this time he refuses Kessel, so Kessel kills him and every single villager anyway. The demon mocks him. There was never a way to win that day. Outside, an aurora lights up the sky and the whole valley seems to lose its mind at once, but Merrin keeps going, and the demon finally leaves Cheche's body. The boy goes back to the way he was before, and Merrin leaves for Rome with his faith restored. One of the elders warns him that the demon will follow him. And twenty-six years later, in Georgetown, it does.

Critics liked this version a little more, and Blatty called it a handsome, classy, elegant piece of work, but almost nobody saw it. In American theaters, it made about two hundred and fifty thousand dollars.

## The Exorcist (TV series, 2016)
There's also a TV show, and it ignores every movie except the first one. In The Exorcist series on Fox in 2016, Geena Davis plays Angela Rance, a mother who's sure something is wrong in her house, and soon one of her daughters, Casey, is possessed. Two priests take the case, a young Father Tomas Ortega and a veteran exorcist, Father Marcus Keane. Then comes the twist. Angela Rance is Regan MacNeil, all grown up and living under a new name, and the demon has come back for her family. The show even brings back Chris MacNeil. It lasted two seasons before it was canceled in 2018.

## The Exorcist: Believer (2023)
In 2021, Universal and Peacock paid four hundred million dollars for a brand new Exorcist trilogy from the team behind the recent Halloween movies. The first one, The Exorcist: Believer, ignores everything except the 1973 original and picks up fifty years later.

It opens in Haiti, where a photographer named Victor Fielding is on his honeymoon with his pregnant wife, Sorenne, when a massive earthquake hits. Sorenne is badly hurt, and the paramedics tell Victor he has to choose, his wife or the baby. Thirteen years later, Victor is raising his daughter Angela alone in Georgia, and he hasn't believed in God since the day Sorenne died.

After school one day, Angela and her best friend Katherine sneak into the woods to hold a séance and try to contact Angela's mother, and they vanish. After a three-day search, the girls are found in a barn, with burns on their feet, and for a moment they seem fine. They're not. Within days both of them are getting worse in ways no doctor can explain.

Victor's neighbor Ann, a nurse who once trained to become a nun, is the first to see it, especially when Angela starts saying things about Ann's past that she couldn't possibly know. Ann gives Victor a book by someone who has lived through this exact nightmare, Chris MacNeil. After Regan's exorcism, Chris spent her life studying exorcisms in every culture and became world famous for her memoir, and because of that book, Regan hasn't seen her since.

Chris, played again by Ellen Burstyn, agrees to help. She tries a ritual on Katherine, and Katherine stabs her in the eyes with a cross. Chris survives, but she'll never see again.

Chris told Victor to use the methods of every culture, so he, Ann and Katherine's parents put together something this franchise has never seen, an exorcism with every faith in the room. There's a Catholic priest named Father Maddox, a Baptist pastor, a Pentecostal preacher and a rootwork healer, until the Church forbids Maddox from taking part, because it thinks the girls just need psychiatric help. During the ritual, the demon tells everyone the secret Victor has kept for thirteen years. In Haiti, he didn't choose the baby. He chose his wife, and she died anyway. Then the demon makes its offer. The parents have to choose which girl lives, or both of them die. Neither family will give up the other's child. Father Maddox changes his mind and comes back, starts reading the ritual, and the demon snaps his neck from across the room. Victor finds his faith again and starts to pray, and then Katherine's father, Tony, breaks and shouts that he chooses Katherine. Angela flatlines. And then the demon reveals the twist. The one you choose is the one who dies. Katherine is dragged down to hell, screaming for her parents, while Angela comes back to life.

In the final scene, Chris is lying in a hospital bed, blind, when someone walks into her room. It's Regan, played again by Linda Blair. After all those years apart, she forgives her mother.

Believer actually opened at number one, but critics hated it, and after that reception the rest of the trilogy was scrapped. The next chapter, The Exorcist: Deceiver, was canceled before it was ever made.

## Outro
So, in fifty years, this demon has possessed a twelve-year-old girl, killed priest after priest, turned into a swarm of locusts in Africa, hidden a serial killer inside a dead priest's body, gotten its own prequel filmed twice, dragged a girl to hell and blinded Chris MacNeil. And every few movies, somebody decides the last ones never happened.

Now it's Mike Flanagan's turn. The Exorcist: Martyrs comes out on March 12, 2027, with Scarlett Johansson as an NYPD homicide detective, Chiwetel Ejiofor as a prison chaplain with a crisis of faith, and John Leguizamo as an infamous satanic serial killer called Father Sunday. It isn't a sequel to Believer. It starts a whole new path, which, honestly, is the most Exorcist thing it could do.
