## s00 cold open

This Freddy will drag you across the ceiling, turn you into a puppet, smash your head through a TV, crush you like a bug, or pull you down into your own bed. And all he needs is for you to fall asleep. We're ranking every Freddy by your chance of surviving him.

## s01 rules

Here's the rule. You fall asleep tonight, and that movie's Freddy is waiting for you. And every Freddy is different. One is so weak he needs Jason to kill for him, one needs a teenager's body, and one isn't even a movie character anymore. So we score each one on strength, intelligence, kill count, and dream power, which is how much he can do to you once your eyes close. Put it all together, and you get one number, your survival rate.

## s02 #9 Freddy vs. Jason (2003), 80%, Don't Worry

Number nine, Freddy versus Jason. This Freddy is the weakest of them all, because his town forgot him. The adults erased every trace of him, and anyone who still dreams about him gets locked away in a hospital and kept on a drug that stops them from dreaming. He's so weak that he has to bring Jason back from Hell to scare people for him. In a movie with his own name on the poster, Jason does nearly all the killing, and Freddy kills exactly one person himself, Mark. In the end, Lori cuts his head off with Jason's machete. If you've never heard his name, he can't even find you. Survival rate, eighty percent.

## s03 #8 Freddy's Revenge (1985), 58%, Close Call

Number eight, Freddy's Revenge. This Freddy doesn't need Jason, because he takes over a teenager named Jesse and climbs right out of his body to kill, which makes him far more dangerous than the last one. He kills Jesse's gym teacher in the showers, then his friend Grady, then a whole group of kids at a pool party, and in the very last scene, his claws burst out of Lisa's friend Kerry on the school bus. But this Freddy does most of his killing in the real world, so you can see him coming and run, and he can be beaten by love. Lisa tells Jesse she loves him, kisses Freddy, and he burns to ash. Stay far away from Jesse, and you've got a decent shot. Survival rate, fifty-eight percent.

## s04 #7 The Dream Child (1989), 50%, Better Run

Number seven, The Dream Child. This Freddy is back in your dreams, and he's found a sneaky new way in, through the dreams of Alice's unborn son, Jacob. That means he can come for her friends even while she's wide awake. He turns Dan into part of his own motorcycle, force-feeds Greta until she chokes, and turns Mark into a paper drawing before slicing him to pieces. But Alice has beaten him once already, and in the end, with help from Freddy's own mother, Jacob uses the power Freddy gave him to destroy him. He only kills three people in this movie, and the kids know how to fight back this time, so it really is a coin flip. Survival rate, fifty percent.

## s05 #6 the 2010 remake, 38%, Almost Dead

Number six, the twenty ten remake. This Freddy is darker, meaner, and not funny at all, and he has a trick that makes staying awake useless. Go too long without sleep, and your brain drops into his world for a few seconds at a time, without you even noticing. By the time Nancy figures out who he is, every other kid from their old preschool is dead except her and Quentin. He gets Dean in a diner, Kris in her bedroom, and Jesse in a jail cell, and even after Nancy drags him into the real world and cuts his throat, he comes back through a mirror for her mother. Staying awake won't save you from this one. Survival rate, thirty-eight percent.

## s06 #5 Freddy's Dead (1991), 30%, Almost Dead

Number five, Freddy's Dead. This Freddy is the silliest one of them all, but don't let the jokes fool you, because he has the biggest body count of any Freddy. Before the movie even starts, he has already killed almost every child and teenager in his town. Then he plays with his victims like toys. He turns up Carlos's hearing until one scratch on a chalkboard kills him, traps Spencer inside a video game, and drops John Doe, the last teenager from Springwood, onto a bed of spikes. It takes his own daughter, Maggie, dragging him into the real world and shoving a pipe bomb into his chest to finally end him. The jokes might buy you a few seconds to run, but this is the Freddy who already wiped out a whole town of kids. Survival rate, thirty percent.

## s07 #4 A Nightmare on Elm Street (1984), 25%, Slaughtered

Number four, the original Nightmare on Elm Street, from nineteen eighty-four. This Freddy barely talks, and that's what makes him scarier than the last one. He stays in the shadows, and nobody believes you until it's too late. He drags Tina across the ceiling, strangles Rod with his own bedsheet in a jail cell, and pulls Glen down into his bed. Nancy only survives because she figures out his rules herself. She sets traps, drags him into the real world, and finally turns her back on him, taking away the fear that feeds him, and even then, he still takes her mother. Unless you're as brave and as smart as Nancy, he gets you. Survival rate, twenty-five percent.

## s08 #3 Dream Warriors (1987), 20%, Slaughtered

Number three, Dream Warriors. This Freddy is deadlier than the last one, because even kids who learn to fight back can't stop him. He hunts the last children of Elm Street in a mental hospital. He turns Phillip into a puppet using his own veins, smashes Jennifer's head into a TV, and kills Taryn and Will even after they find their dream powers. Then his skeleton kills Nancy's father in the real world, and Freddy kills Nancy herself while pretending to be her dad. The kids who made it had superpowers, and it still took holy water on his bones to stop him. You don't have superpowers. Survival rate, twenty percent.

## s09 #2 The Dream Master (1988), 12%, Slaughtered

Number two, The Dream Master. This Freddy is the one who wiped out the Dream Warriors. Kincaid, Joey and Kristen, the kids who beat the last Freddy, are all dead early in the movie. Then he goes through Alice's friends one by one, Sheila, Rick, and Debbie, who he turns into a cockroach and crushes, and every soul he takes stays trapped inside him. Alice only wins by taking on her dead friends' powers and showing Freddy his own reflection, so the souls inside him break free and tear him apart. If the kids with superpowers couldn't survive this one, you won't either. Survival rate, twelve percent.

## s10 #1 Wes Craven's New Nightmare (1994), 8%, Slaughtered

And number one, New Nightmare. This Freddy isn't a movie character at all. It's something ancient that the films had kept trapped, and when the series ended, it broke loose. It goes after Heather Langenkamp, the actress who played Nancy, because killing her is how it gets into the real world. It kills two special effects men working on a new Freddy movie, then her husband while he's driving, then her son's babysitter in a hospital, and then it takes her little boy. To stop it, Heather has to become Nancy one last time and, together with her son, push it into a furnace. Every other Freddy has rules you can learn, but this one is real, so none of those rules will save you. Survival rate, eight percent.
